What is machine learning, really?

No formulas. One business problem, three graphs, one big idea.

A phone company is losing customers.

Customers

7,043

Left last year

The manager asks

"Which customers will leave next month?"

The team can make only 200 calls a month.

Why? Keeping a customer is much cheaper than finding a new one. With only 200 calls, they must call the right 200.

First: look at the data.

Pattern 1: the contract

Why? A monthly customer can leave any day with no penalty. A two-year customer already committed.

Pattern 2: new customers leave most

Why? New customers are still testing the service. Long-time customers already proved they are happy.

Could we just write rules?

IF   contract is month-to-month
AND  customer for less than 6 months
THEN call them

That works a little. Now look at two things at once…

Each dot = one customer

Red dots crowd top-left: new customers with high bills. But there is no clean line.

Why hand-written rules fail

1 People can't juggle 19 signals at once
2 Patterns change, rules go out of date
3 Nobody knows how much each signal should count

How does a computer learn?

Like a student with an answer key

1 · Guess"this customer stays"
→
2 · Checkanswer: they left ✗
→
3 · Adjustcount "new" more

↺ repeat over all 7,043 customers, many times

Every model in this course, and every LLM, learns with this loop.

One more trick: hide some examples

Learn from these · 80%
Hidden test · 20%
Why? A student who memorised the answer key scores 100% without understanding. Only questions they have never seen show if they truly learned.

Three ways to learn

📝

With answers

Supervised. Our churn problem.

🧩

Without answers

Unsupervised. Find customer groups on its own.

🎮

By reward

Reinforcement. Points for good actions: games, robots.

So where do ChatGPT and Claude fit?

They learned by predicting the next word.

Why? The text itself is the answer key, so no one had to label it (self-supervised). Then reward-based training taught them to be helpful.
