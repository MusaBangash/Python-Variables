Failure 2 · Overfitting

The model memorises instead of learning.

On customers it learned from

99.8%

73.4%
Why? With no limits, the tree built 1,102 tiny rules for 5,634 customers: almost one rule per small group. It memorised them, noise included.

Near-perfect in training. Worse than a blind guess in reality.

The sweet spot is in the middle

The ruleJudge a model only on data it never saw.
The training score tells you almost nothing.

The expert names: bias and variance

Plain meaningOur churn exampleSign you see
Biaswrong in the same way, every time: the model is too simple1-question treebad on training and unseen data
Variancechanges a lot when the data changes: the model is too flexibleunlimited treegreat on training, bad on unseen
Noiserandomness no model can removea happy customer who moves citya floor no model gets below
Why? For squared-error problems the expected error splits exactly into bias² + variance + noise. Making a model more flexible lowers bias but raises variance, so you look for the balance, not zero.

Part B

Is your test itself honest?

Same model. 30 random splits. 30 different scores.

Why? Which 1,409 customers land in the test set changes the score by up to 3 points. One split can make a model look better or worse than it is.

All the ways to test, and when each is right

MethodHow it worksUse it when
Single holdoutone train / test cuthuge data, or the final check only
K-fold cross-validationk cuts; every row is tested once; average the k scoressmall or medium data, comparing models
Stratified k-foldk-fold that keeps the class ratio (26.5% churn) in every foldclassification with uneven classes
Repeated k-foldk-fold several times with new shufflestwo models are very close
Group k-foldall rows of one customer stay togethermany rows per customer or patient
Time-based splitlearn on the past, test on the futureanything predicted forward in time
Nested CVan inner CV tunes, an outer CV scorestuning and honest scoring on small data

So which one for our churn product?

1 Lock away a final test set first (20%) and never use it for decisions
2 Compare and tune with stratified 5-fold CV on the rest
3 In production: a time-based check: train on past months, test on next month
Why? We predict the future, so the most honest test imitates the future. Our IBM snapshot has no dates, which is a real limitation to state in any interview.

Three sets, three jobs

SetIts only jobIf you misuse it
Trainingthe model learns its rules here-
Validation (or CV)choose between models and settingsfine: this is its purpose
TestONE final, honest numberuse it to choose, and its number becomes too optimistic
Why? Every decision made by looking at a set "uses up" its honesty. That is why the test set is opened once, at the end. Peeking early is called data snooping.

2.4 million answers. Wrong winner.

Why? In 1936 the Literary Digest poll predicted Landon would beat Roosevelt. Its lists (phone books, magazine subscribers) favoured richer voters, and fewer than 25% replied. Roosevelt won with 62%. Big data from the wrong people is still wrong.

Best choice here: our 11 blanks all have 0 months of tenure: they haven't been billed yet, so the true value is 0. Investigate before you fill.

The most dangerous data problem

Leakage: the answer hides inside a feature.

Three kinds of leakage

TypeWhat happensExamplePrevention
Target leakagea feature is created after, or because of, the outcome"offer after cancel request"ask: is this known at prediction time?
Train-test contaminationtest data influences training stepsscaling or filling blanks using all data before splittingsplit first; fit every step on training data only (pipelines)
Time leakagethe future is used to predict the pastrandom split of monthly datatime-based splits
Why? Leakage is why a too-good-to-be-true score should make you suspicious, not happy.

Even a perfect model ages: data shift

ShiftPlain meaningChurn example
Covariate shiftthe customers changea new cheap plan attracts younger customers
Label (prior) shifthow often the answer happens changesa competitor's promo doubles churn for a month
Concept driftthe link between features and answer changescontracts stop mattering after a policy change
Why? A model is a photo of the past. When the world moves, the photo goes stale. That is why production systems monitor and retrain (later lessons).
No Free Lunch theoremNo model is best for every problem.
You must test to know which wins on yours.

Check yourself · 1 of 5

Train 99.8%, unseen 73.4%. What is happening, and what do you try first?

AnswerOverfitting (high variance). First: limit the model (e.g. max depth), then regularize or use an ensemble.

Check yourself · 2 of 5

Both training and unseen scores are 74%. Will more data help?

AnswerUnlikely: lines that meet low mean the model is too simple. Add better features or a stronger model.

Check yourself · 3 of 5

Two models: 80.4% vs 80.1% on one split. Which is better?

AnswerYou can't tell. Splits alone moved scores by ~3 points. Use repeated, stratified CV and compare the spread.

Check yourself · 4 of 5

A new feature lifts accuracy from 80% to 94%. Your first question?

Answer"Is this value known at prediction time?" Suspect leakage before celebrating.

Check yourself · 5 of 5

Why open the test set only once?

AnswerEvery decision made by looking at it makes its score more optimistic: data snooping.
RememberToo simple and memorising are the two failures.
An honest test catches both,
unless the data itself is lying.
